What it does

Built for the twelve seconds between plays.

Everything here exists because of one constraint: you are standing on a sideline, the offense is already breaking the huddle, and you can't afford to look down for long.

Log at game speed

Formation, play, ball carrier, a spot button, save. That's a plain run in about five taps, and when you're done the ball is already placed for the next snap. The keyboard never appears unless you go looking for the notes field.

Stats while it still matters

Totals update as the plays go in, so you can answer "how many carries has he got?" in the third quarter instead of on Saturday morning with a spreadsheet and a legal pad.

Mistakes don't wreck the game

Undo the last play, or open one from the middle of the log and change where it ended. Every play after it repairs itself. Numbering, drives and start spots all follow along.

Nothing goes unscored

Reach the end zone and the dashboard asks for the points and the try, already filled in. Opponent scores and kickoff returns are handled too, and a return won't ask you for a formation you never called.

No bars, no problem

Some of these stadiums have no signal at all, and the app is built not to care. Log a full game offline: plays, scoring, all of it. It catches up the moment the connection comes back.

The whole staff, one game

Everyone signed in to your team works the same game together. Hand parents and players the invite code and they join read-only. They see the score, the play-by-play and the stats, never the playbook.

How it works

Set it up once in August. Use it every Friday after that.

Build your team

Enter your roster: name, number, position, grade. Then your formations and play calls, and your team colors. Then send your staff the invite code so everybody's in.

Work the game

Log plays as they happen. The score, the down and distance and the ball position keep themselves current, and the stats build in the background while you coach.

Send the report

When it's over, open the postgame report. Line score, play-by-play, player totals. Share it as text or as a PDF without waiting until you're home.

A look at it

Big type, high contrast, readable in the sun.

On an iPad in landscape it splits in two: entry on the left, the play log on the right.

On student information

Your roster is your roster.

The app holds information about minors, so it's worth being plain about it. Roster data is visible only to the people your team's owner has let in. It is never sold, never shared with other companies, never used for advertising, and never used to track anyone anywhere. There is no analytics or tracking software in the app of any kind.
