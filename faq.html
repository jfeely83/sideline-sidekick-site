Coach's FAQ

The things staffs ask before they'll put a season into it.

Getting started

What is this, exactly?

An iOS app for high school football coaching staffs. Somebody on the sideline logs each play as it happens; the app keeps the score, the down and distance and the ball position current, builds player statistics as it goes, and produces a postgame report when the game ends.

It's meant to replace the clipboard and the spreadsheet. It isn't a film system or a scouting service.

What does it cost?

Nothing while it's in beta. Pricing after that hasn't been settled. If it does change, testers will hear it from me first, not from an App Store receipt.

What do I need to run it?

An iPhone or an iPad. Most of the staffs using it are on iPads, and it's built for both. In landscape on an iPad the screen splits in two, with entry on the left and the play log on the right.

There is no Android version, and no web version.

How do I get it?

Email support@sideline-sidekick.com and say you're interested in the beta, and I'll get you signed up.

It runs through TestFlight, Apple's beta program. You'll get an invite by email, install TestFlight if you don't already have it, and the app installs from there.

Can I look around before I type in a whole roster?

Yes. There's a demo login with an invented school and a game already loaded. Email support@sideline-sidekick.com and ask for it.

One warning: everybody shares that one demo account, so if two people are poking at it simultaneously you'll be logging plays into the same game as a stranger. It's fine for a look around and no good for a real test.

Setting up your team

What do I have to enter before the first game?

Three things, and only once. Your roster: name, number, position, grade. Your playbook, meaning the formations and calls you actually use. And your team colors. A team logo if you want one.

After that it's set. You're not rebuilding anything week to week.

Can my whole staff be in it, or just me?

The whole staff. You create the team, then share the invite code; anyone signed in as a member of that team can log plays into the same game. That means one coach can spot while another logs, and neither of you is retyping the other's work.

Can parents and players follow along?

If you want them to. Anyone you give the invite code to joins in a restricted Fan role: they can see the score, the play-by-play and player statistics, and that's all. A Fan can't change anything, and can't see your formations or play names.

Whether the code goes out that widely is entirely the team owner's call. Treat it the way you'd treat any other roster information.

What if somebody leaves the staff?

The team owner can remove any member at any time from the Coaches screen. Ownership can also be handed to another coach.

On game day

How long does logging a play actually take?

A plain run is about five taps: formation, play, ball carrier, a spot button, save. When you hit save the ball is already placed for the next snap, so you're not resetting anything between downs.

The keyboard is deliberately kept out of your way. It doesn't appear anywhere except the notes field.

There's no cell signal at half the places we play.

That's an assumption the app was built on, not an edge case. You can log a complete game with the device in airplane mode: plays, scoring, opponent scores, all of it. It syncs back up when a connection returns. It shouldn't feel any slower offline.

I logged something wrong. Did I just ruin the game record?

No. You can undo the last play, or open any play in the middle of the log and change it, including where it ended. Everything after it repairs itself: play numbers, drives and start spots all re-thread automatically.

Can a touchdown go unrecorded?

When a play reaches the end zone, a prompt appears on the dashboard asking you to add the points and the try. Tap it and the sheet is already filled in. You just answer the try.

It doesn't hijack the screen mid-drive, so it doesn't cost you the next snap. Opponent scores and kickoff returns are handled too, and a return won't ask you for a formation you never called.

Can I read it outside in the sun?

It's designed for it. Big type, heavy contrast, and a play log meant to be read at a glance rather than studied. If something washes out on your device on a bright afternoon, that's a bug worth reporting, not something to work around.

After the game

What's in the postgame report?

The line score by quarter, the play-by-play, and player statistics. You can share it as plain text, which drops straight into an email or a message, or as a PDF.

You can send it from the parking lot. It doesn't wait on you getting home to a computer.

Can I get the data out?

The report shares as text or PDF today. If you need it in another form, say a spreadsheet or a particular stat service's format, put that in an email. It's exactly the kind of thing the beta is for.

Roster information and privacy

Who can see our roster?

Only people the team's owner has admitted to that team. That means your coaching staff, plus anyone in the read-only Fan role if the owner has shared the invite code that far. Nobody else.

Those limits are enforced on the server, not only in the app.

Is any of it sold, shared or used for advertising?

No. Roster and team information is never sold or rented, never shared with other companies, never used for advertising or profiling, and never used to track anyone across other apps or websites. The app contains no analytics or tracking software of any kind.

Where is the data actually stored?

On Google Cloud, through Firebase Authentication and Cloud Firestore. Google acts as the hosting provider; the data isn't shared with Google for its own advertising or marketing. The app also keeps an offline copy on your device so it works on a field with no reception.

What about our district's rules on student information?

Those are yours to follow, and the app doesn't override them. The recommendation is to enter the minimum you need to run a game: name, number, position, grade, and nothing more.

The privacy policy spells out exactly what is stored and who can reach it, in plain language, if you need to hand it to an athletic director.

How do we delete things?

A player comes off the roster with a swipe. A game deletes with every play and score in it. A whole team can be deleted by its owner, who has to type the team's name to confirm. That one is permanent, and it takes the roster, playbook and every game with it. You can also delete your own account entirely.

Step-by-step instructions are in the privacy policy. If you'd rather not do it yourself, or you can't sign in, email support@sideline-sidekick.com from the address on the account.

Getting help

Something's broken, or I want it to do something it doesn't.

Email support@sideline-sidekick.com. Include the version you're running and, if it's a bug, what you were doing when it happened. Replies usually come within a few days.

Anything that made you pause, re-read or tap twice is worth sending. You don't need a reason attached. "I couldn't find it" and "I didn't trust that number" are useful on their own. If a statistic looks wrong, that's the most valuable thing you can report.

Who's behind this?

Jeffrey Feely. One person, coaching and building it at the same time. That's the reason it's shaped the way it is. Everything in it exists because it was needed on an actual sideline on an actual Friday.

Still got a question?

Ask it directly. It's a short chain of command around here.
